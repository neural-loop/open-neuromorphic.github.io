It has been an incredibly busy few months behind the scenes at Open Neuromorphic! As our community has grown to over 3,100 members, the demand for structured resources, open science validation, and accessible educational pathways has skyrocketed.

We have spent the summer analyzing ecosystem surveys, speaking with academic labs, and looking closely at how our community collaborates. Today, we are excited to announce a massive slate of strategic initiatives designed to scale our infrastructure and support the next wave of neuromorphic innovation.

Gauging your response to these initiatives will help us chart our path forward. We want to know what you care about most, and we are actively looking for volunteers to help bring these visions to life.

Here is a look at what is currently underway.

## 1. The Relaunch of Open Neuromorphic Research (ONR 2.0)

We are fundamentally re-architecting the Open Neuromorphic Research (ONR) program. To combat reviewer fatigue and the barriers of closed-door publishing, ONR 2.0 pioneers a **"Process as Provenance"** model. By conducting research in the open on ONM infrastructure, the process itself becomes part of the scientific record.

We are deeply focused on supporting **reproducible research** and building clear "on-ramps" for people at all levels of research experience. This means establishing diversified publication tracks—from short papers to hands-on tutorials and meta-essays—so anyone from a student to a senior PI has a place to contribute.

A huge thank you to Voting Member **Michael Furlong**, who has been instrumental in conceptualizing and driving the scientific vision for ONR 2.0, while the Executive Committee works to build the backend infrastructure and partnerships to make it an operational reality.

Alongside this, we are launching an automated discovery feed to highlight open-access papers, expanding our Collaboration Network, and conducting direct outreach to academic labs to build new research cooperations and define our Steering Committee.

👉 **Track our progress & read the architecture:** [Issue #131: Revitalise ONR (Open Neuromorphic Research)](https://github.com/open-neuromorphic/communications/issues/131)

## 2. Education: "Zero to Silicon" Program Development

To bridge the gap between traditional software engineering and neuromorphic hardware, we are advancing our "Zero to Silicon" educational pipeline.

Based on community feedback and proven pedagogical strategies, we are shifting the curriculum toward interactive Jupyter/Colab notebooks, hands-on tutorials, and mini-research projects. The goal is to make concepts like spiking neural networks and digital logic mapping highly accessible, without requiring participants to install complex local toolchains.

👉 **Check out the roadmap:** [Issue #146: Zero to Silicon - Content Scaffolding & Format Adoption](https://github.com/open-neuromorphic/communications/issues/146)

## 3. The Master Taxonomy Initiative

With so much hardware, software, and research content, finding the exact tool or community group for a specific use case can be challenging. To tie our ecosystem together, we just launched a major initiative to define ONM’s **Content Taxonomy**.

This shared vocabulary will separate the technical nature of a tool (Expertise) from its intended use case (Application). This will allow us to overhaul the Hardware and Software guides with powerful filters and launch dedicated "Niche Hub" landing pages for specific fields (like Robotics, Medicine, or Space).

👉 **Help us define the categories:** [Issue #154: Defining and Implementing the ONM Content Taxonomy](https://github.com/open-neuromorphic/communications/issues/154)

## 4. Defining ONM's Strategy for Standardized Benchmarking

In our recent ecosystem survey, standardized benchmarking emerged as one of the most critical demands from institutional and enterprise stakeholders. The field needs a clear, vendor-neutral way to evaluate energy efficiency, accuracy, and tooling.

But what should ONM's primary role be? Should we host an active leaderboard and run tests ourselves? Or should we act as a curation hub for existing evaluation frameworks? We are actively seeking your input to ensure our benchmarking efforts remain 100% open-source, fully reproducible, and rigorously vendor-neutral.

👉 **Join the discussion on GitHub:** [Issue #153: Defining ONM's Strategy for Standardized Benchmarking & Open Evaluation](https://github.com/open-neuromorphic/communications/issues/153)
